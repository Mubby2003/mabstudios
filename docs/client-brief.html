01 What the site is for

This decides everything else. A site built to win wedding bookings looks nothing like one built to show a portfolio.

  1. If the site does one job well in its first year, what is that job? Booked weddings, studio sessions, brand clients, or simply looking established?
  2. How do people find and book you today — Instagram DMs, WhatsApp, word of mouth, referrals?
  3. What do you want the site to change about that?
  4. Roughly how many bookings a year would make this worth it?
  5. Is there a type of work you want more of, or any you want to stop being asked for?

02 Who it is for

Couples planning a wedding, a founder needing headshots and a mother booking a baby shoot want completely different things from the same page.

  1. Describe your ideal client. Age, where they live, what the occasion is.
  2. What are they nervous about before they book a photographer?
  3. What question do you get asked most often before someone books?
  4. Which other photographers do clients compare you against? Links welcome.
  5. Do you serve one area or travel? Are there cities or venues worth naming?

03 What makes you different

This becomes the words on the page. Written in your voice it reads as you; written in mine it reads as a template.

  1. How would you describe your work to a friend, out loud, in one sentence?
  2. What do clients say afterwards — is there a compliment you hear repeatedly?
  3. What do you do that other studios in your area do not?
  4. Being both British and Nigerian in your work — how much should the site lead with that?
  5. Any words you want on the site, or words you would never use?

04 What we need from you

This is the part that decides how fast the site gets finished. Everything else I can build; only you can supply these.

  • Photographs — the big one.40–60 images if possible. JPEG, long edge 2400px, quality 80, sRGB. Straight from Lightroom, not downloaded from Instagram. Include some landscape frames and some detail shots, not only portraits.
  • Two or three complete shootsA whole wedding or session, start to finish. Couples want proof you can cover a full day, not a highlights reel.
  • Client reviewsReal quotes with a first name and the type of shoot. Screenshots from WhatsApp or Instagram are fine — I will transcribe them.
  • Prices, or a starting priceEven "weddings from £X" changes results. Hiding prices is the most common reason people leave a photographer's site.
  • Logo filesSVG or PNG with a transparent background, if a logo exists.
  • A short about paragraphWho you are and how you got here. Voice notes are fine — I will write it up.
  • A photograph of youPeople book a person, not a studio.
  • Business details to publishContact email, phone, service area, company number and VAT number if registered.
  • Model permissionConfirmation that the people in these photographs are happy to appear on a public website.
The single biggest lever. The site currently runs on twelve images copied from Instagram at 640 pixels. Full-resolution originals will improve it more than any redesign, effect or new section.

05 What goes on it

So we agree the shape before anything is built, rather than adding pages later.

  1. Is one long page enough, or do you want separate pages for Weddings, Portraits and Events?
  2. Do you want prices listed, or an enquiry-only approach?
  3. Should there be a journal or blog? Useful for search, but only if you will write it.
  4. Do you need private client galleries — password-protected sets for delivering photographs?
  5. Should people be able to book and pay a deposit online, or just enquire?
  6. An FAQ page? It answers the questions from section 02 and saves you repeating yourself.

06 How it should feel

  1. Three photography websites you like — and specifically what you like about each.
  2. One you dislike, and why.
  3. Dark and cinematic, or light and editorial?
  4. How much movement do you want? Animation can feel expensive or it can feel like it is trying too hard.
  5. Any colours tied to your brand already, from cards, prints or your logo?

07 The practical side

  1. Who should own the domain and hosting accounts? My recommendation is that you do, with me added as a collaborator — so nothing depends on me being reachable.
  2. Which inbox should enquiries arrive in?
  3. Do you want to update the site yourself — adding photographs without asking me? This is the main thing that decides how it gets built.
  4. Do you need Google Analytics, or is knowing enquiries arrive enough?
  5. Are you registered with Google Business Profile? It matters more than the website for local searches.
  6. Do you have a privacy policy? A contact form collecting names and emails needs one under UK GDPR.

08 Timing and money

  1. Is there a date this needs to be live — a wedding fair, a season, a campaign?
  2. What budget range are you working to?
  3. Who signs off? If it is more than one person, say so now.
  4. After launch, do you want ongoing help — updates, new galleries, fixes — or hand it over entirely?
